# Draft Readiness

Run this after reading the conversation and before writing the draft. Surface-specific skills still
govern turn-taking, delivery, and authorization; this skill owns the pre-draft judgment gate.

## Refresh the premise

Re-read the live conversation and refresh anything that can expire: meeting status, dates,
delivery timing, approvals, product decisions, current owners, and work in progress. A draft is
not ready while its premise may be stale.

## Map the decision state

Classify every material claim and next step:

| State | Drafting treatment |
|---|---|
| `settled` | State it directly and retain the supporting source. |
| `pending` | Name the dependency; ask the narrow open question or use timing that allows it to close. |
| `tentative` | Preserve the hypothesis and confidence level. Do not turn it into a correction or commitment. |
| `unknown` | Retrieve the missing fact when an authoritative source exists. Otherwise ask for it plainly. |

Internal estimates become external dates only after they are an approved external position.
Provisional workarounds stay out of stakeholder guidance while a definitive fix is moving. Work
becomes review-ready only after its scope, naming, decisions, and required validation are stable.

## Pass the source gate

Use the authoritative source available for the claim: current product documentation, a controlling
communication, a domain skill, or observed runtime evidence. Use a known answer instead of a
placeholder follow-up. When no authoritative answer is available, state the exact unresolved point
instead of hedging around missing retrieval.

## Ready condition

The premise is current; every material assertion has a decision state and adequate source; every
dependency is visible; and the draft contains no unsupported commitment, estimate, workaround, or
claim of readiness.
